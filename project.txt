[S01] VISION, PROBLEM, POSITIONING
================================================================================

01.1  THE OBSERVED PROBLEM
      Most school and college students do not study steadily. They study
      intensely the LAST DAY before the exam. On that day they:
        - do not know which topics actually matter,
        - waste hours on low-value topics because they are "interesting",
        - panic, jump between topics, and finish nothing,
        - have no notes they trust, and no time to make them,
        - sleep too little, which destroys recall.
      Existing tools (generic AI tutors, YouTube, textbooks) assume a student with
      weeks of time. Nobody designs for the real behavior: last-day learning.

01.2  THE PRODUCT IN ONE SENTENCE
      SkillOS is an AI learning operating system that knows each student
      (Learning Twin), plans their journey, finds the best resources, teaches,
      evaluates, and rebuilds the path continuously - and its flagship mode,
      EXAM EVE RESCUE, turns "I have 1 day and I know nothing" into a
      personalized, calm, hour-by-hour plan with notes and a cheat sheet
      that maximizes the student's chance of passing.

01.3  TWO LAYERS OF THE PRODUCT
      Layer A - FLAGSHIP (what judges and first-time users see):
                Exam Eve Rescue. Upload syllabus/PYQs -> get Pass Core topics,
                hour-by-hour plan, notes, cheat sheet, quizzes.
      Layer B - PLATFORM (the long-term moat):
                Learning Twin, adaptive roadmap, resource discovery graph,
                mistake intelligence, gamified quest map, knowledge base,
                community-submitted resources, per-paragraph translation.
      Rule: Layer A must work end-to-end BEFORE any Layer B module is started,
      except the Learning Twin (shared foundation).

01.4  POSITIONING LINE
      "Same exam. Different student. Different plan."
      Secondary: "Don't study everything. Study what gets you through."

01.5  DIFFERENTIATORS (memorize for pitch)
      D1  Designed for last-day reality, not idealized study habits.
      D2  Pass-Core triage: mathematically picks the minimum topic set that
          reaches the pass mark plus a safety margin (see S12).
      D3  Personalization by behavior (focus span, speed, chronotype, panic
          level), not just by topic knowledge.
      D4  PYQ-driven prediction with transparent evidence ("appeared in 7 of
          9 papers").
      D5  Anti-overwhelm UX: one task at a time, Panic Mode, protected sleep.
      D6  Notes + cheat sheet generated from THE STUDENT'S OWN uploaded material
          with page citations (not generic internet text).
      D7  Honest confidence: shows Pass Confidence with a range and reasons;
          never promises a guaranteed pass.
      D8  Gets smarter every exam (post-exam feedback loop updates Twin + PYQ
          database).

01.6  NON-GOALS (say no to these)
      - Not a full LMS or a school-admin tool.
      - Not a system that helps students cheat inside the exam hall (see S24).
      - Not a "guaranteed pass" product; we maximize probability and say so.
      - No 20-microservice architecture. One Next.js app + Supabase + workers.

[S02] USERS AND PERSONAS
================================================================================

02.1  PRIMARY USER: the Last-Day Learner
      Age 14-24. School (grades 8-12), undergraduate (engineering, arts,
      science, commerce), diploma/polytechnic, competitive-exam beginners.
      Uses a phone first, laptop second. Studies late at night. Unreliable
      internet at times. May prefer regional language explanations (e.g.
      Malayalam, Hindi, Tamil) but English technical terms.

02.2  PERSONAS (use these in design reviews and in the demo)
      P1  "Anu" - Engineering sem-3 student. Exam in 16 hours. Has last 5 years
          of PYQs as PDFs. Strong in theory, weak in numericals. Focus span 25
          min. Panics after 10 pm. Needs: Pass Core + formulas cheat sheet.
      P2  "Rahul" - Grade 12 commerce. Exam in 20 hours. Has textbook photos
          only. Learns best from examples. Phone-only. Needs: image OCR,
          simple language, mnemonics, Malayalam translation on tap.
      P3  "Meera" - Final-year degree student. 3 exams in 3 days. Needs: multi-
          exam allocation (V1). Consistent but time-poor.
      P4  "Kabir" - Skipped the whole semester. Zero knowledge. Needs: honest
          "Survival mode" plan, not false hope; lowest-depth coverage of
          highest-frequency topics.
      P5  "Teacher/Mentor" (V2) - shares a syllabus/PYQ pack with a class.

02.3  EMOTIONAL STATE MODEL
      Arrival state is usually: anxious, guilty, overwhelmed.
      Product tone: calm, direct, non-judgmental, encouraging, short sentences.
      Never shame ("you should have started earlier"). Say: "Here is what we can
      do with the time you have."

[S03] FLAGSHIP: EXAM EVE RESCUE (PRODUCT DEFINITION)
================================================================================

03.1  DEFINITION
      A guided flow that takes a student with limited time before an exam and
      produces, within about 3 minutes of wall-clock effort:
        (1) Pass Core map      - which topics matter most and why
        (2) Rescue Plan        - hour-by-hour timeline personalized to behavior
        (3) Rescue Notes       - layered notes per topic (30-sec / 3-min / deep)
        (4) Cheat Sheet        - 1-2 page condensed revision sheet per subject
        (5) Flashcards + Quick Quizzes per topic, auto-graded
        (6) Predicted questions + answer templates (2 / 5 / 10 mark structure)
        (7) Pass Confidence meter that updates as the student progresses
        (8) Calm layer         - Now/Next/Later view, Panic Mode, protected sleep

03.2  MODES BY TIME REMAINING (T = hours until exam start)
      EMERGENCY  T < 4    : Pass Core L1 only, cheat sheet, flashcards, no deep
                            notes, no full sleep block (do a 20-min nap max).
      SPRINT     4 <= T < 10 : Pass Core L1 + selective L2, short sleep block
                            (min 4.5h if T allows at all; else warn).
      STANDARD   10 <= T < 30 : full flow (the "1 day before" case). DEFAULT.
      EXTENDED   30 <= T <= 72 : multi-day plan, day-by-day, each day is a
                            STANDARD-like plan; Day-last is the "eve" plan.
      T > 72 h   : redirect to normal adaptive roadmap (Layer B).

03.3  INPUTS THE STUDENT CAN GIVE (any combination; all optional except exam
      date/time and subject)
      I1  Exam name, subject, domain/board/university/course
      I2  Exam date + start time, duration
      I3  Syllabus (PDF, image, pasted text, or typed topic list)
      I4  Previous-year question papers (PYQ) - PDFs, images, links, pasted text
      I5  Class notes, textbook chapters, PPTs (PDF/image/text)
      I6  Exam pattern (sections, marks, choice rules, negative marking)
      I7  Target: "just pass" (default) / 60% / 75% / custom
      I8  Self-report on study behavior (the 8-question behavior check, S09)
      I9  Optional: past-performance snapshot (last internal marks, weak subject)
      Missing input handling:
        - No syllabus + no PYQ: agent proposes topic list from curriculum
          library for the given board/course and labels it "UNVERIFIED - please
          confirm". Student can edit. Pass Confidence is capped at MEDIUM.
        - No exam pattern: assume typical pattern for the board/course from
          library, label as assumption.

03.4  OUTPUT SCREENS (see S21 for wireframes)
      O1  "Your Rescue Plan is ready" summary card: Pass Confidence, hours,
          number of Pass Core topics, what we will skip, sleep protected.
      O2  Timeline (Now / Next / Later default; full timeline on demand)
      O3  Topic cards (Pass Core, Should-do, Nice-to-have, Skipped-with-reason)
      O4  Notes reader with 3 depth layers and per-paragraph translate/simplify
      O5  Cheat sheet viewer + PDF export (print-friendly and phone-friendly)
      O6  Quiz/flashcard runner
      O7  Progress + Confidence live meter
      O8  Panic button (always visible)

03.5  DESIGN PRINCIPLES (non-negotiable)
      DP1  One primary action on screen at a time.
      DP2  Show at most 3 upcoming tasks. Everything else collapsed.
      DP3  Every plan block has a duration and a clear "done" condition.
      DP4  Protect sleep. If time is short, cut content, not sleep (below the
           minimum viable sleep, warn clearly; do not silently remove).
      DP5  Explain the "why" of each priority in one line with evidence
           ("Appeared in 6/8 papers, avg 10 marks").
      DP6  Everything generated from student's own documents cites doc + page.
      DP7  Every claim of prediction is labeled as an estimate.
      DP8  Offline-friendly: after plan generation, plan + notes + cheat sheet
           + flashcards must work without internet (PWA cache).
      DP9  Language: English default; translate/simplify per paragraph on tap.
      DP10 No dark patterns, no streak-shaming, no fake urgency.

03.6  THE "CHEAT SHEET" FEATURE - PRODUCT FRAMING (important)
      The cheat sheet is a CONDENSED REVISION SHEET for the last hours before
      the exam: formulas, definitions, mnemonics, diagrams, answer skeletons,
      common traps, and a "panic card" of the 10 most important lines.
      UI copy and Terms state: it is a study aid to memorize from; it is not
      for use inside an examination hall. (See S24.) Internally the feature key
      is `cheatsheet`; user-facing label can be "Cheat Sheet" or "Rescue Sheet".

03.7  HONESTY CONTRACT
      - Pass Confidence is an ESTIMATE from PYQ patterns, time available, and
        the student's measured behavior. Always show range + drivers.
      - If the target is unrealistic for the time left, say so plainly and
        show the best achievable option ("With 6 hours, realistic target is
        'borderline pass'. Here is the safest way to use it.").
      - Never state "guaranteed pass".

[S04] SCOPE: MVP / V1 / V2
================================================================================

04.1  MVP (hackathon) - MUST WORK END TO END
      M1  Auth (email/Google via Supabase Auth)
      M2  Rescue intake form + 8-question behavior check + 5-question
          micro-diagnostic on top topics
      M3  Upload: PDF (text-based) + image (OCR) + pasted text; PYQ + syllabus +
          notes
      M4  Ingestion: parse -> chunk -> embed -> topic extraction -> PYQ mapping
      M5  Triage engine with Pass Core selection + Pass Confidence
      M6  Planner producing hour-by-hour plan (STANDARD + EMERGENCY modes)
      M7  Notes (3 layers) + Cheat Sheet (HTML + PDF export) for Pass Core topics
      M8  Flashcards + quick quiz with auto-grading + mistake tagging
      M9  Live adaptation: replan on overrun or weak quiz result; Panic Mode
      M10 Per-paragraph translate (Malayalam, Hindi + English simplify)
      M11 Learning Twin (basic): stores behavior metrics, updates after session
      M12 Demo mode with two seeded students (Anu vs Rahul) - see S26
      M13 PWA offline cache for plan, notes, cheat sheet, flashcards

04.2  V1 (after hackathon, first 6-8 weeks)
      - Multi-exam allocation (3 exams in 3 days)
      - Resource Discovery Engine (web + community resources ranked per student)
      - Community submissions with reputation
      - Full Adaptive Roadmap (long-term path) and gamified Quest Map
      - Weekly revision digest (spaced repetition)
      - WhatsApp/Telegram reminder channel
      - Voice mode (read the cheat sheet aloud)

04.3  V2
      - Teacher/class packs; institution dashboards
      - Docker code sandbox for programming/CTF labs
      - Handwritten note OCR at higher accuracy
      - Post-exam result loop to calibrate predictor per university
      - Regional-language full UI

04.4  CUT LINE RULE
      If time runs short, cut in this order (last cut first):
        M13 offline -> M10 translate -> M12 second demo student -> M9 replan
        (keep Panic Mode) -> M8 quiz grading (keep flashcards).
      NEVER cut: M3, M4, M5, M6, M7 (this is the flagship).

[S05] SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

05.1  HIGH-LEVEL DIAGRAM

     Browser / PWA (Next.js, TS, Tailwind, shadcn/ui)
        |   HTTPS (REST + SSE streaming)         ^ Supabase Realtime (plan updates)
        v                                        |
     Next.js Route Handlers / Server Actions  (thin API layer, auth check, Zod)
        |                      |                       |
        |                      v                       v
        |              Job queue (jobs table)    Supabase Storage (uploads, PDFs)
        |                      |
        v                      v
     Postgres (Supabase)   WORKER (Node, long-running or Edge Function)
       - relational data      - ingestion pipeline (S10)
       - pgvector chunks      - agent runner (calls LLM provider)
       - RLS security         - PDF generation (headless Chromium)
                              - scheduled jobs (reminders, weekly digest)
                                      |
                                      v
                              LLM provider abstraction (default: OpenAI API)
                              + OCR (Tesseract or vision-LLM) + Web search API
                                (V1) + Translation via LLM

05.2  ORCHESTRATION MODEL
      One ORCHESTRATOR (R0) executes a "run graph" per user action. Each node is
      a runtime agent (R1..R14) with typed input/output (Zod schemas). The
      orchestrator stores every step in `agent_runs` for traceability, retry,
      cost tracking and debugging.

05.3  KEY DESIGN RULES
      A1  Deterministic code for math (triage, scheduling, scoring). LLMs for
          language, extraction and judgment. NEVER let an LLM do arithmetic for
          the schedule; the LLM proposes, the planner code computes and validates.
      A2  Every LLM call returns JSON validated by Zod. On invalid JSON: retry
          once with a repair prompt, then fall back to a safe default.
      A3  Long jobs are async. UI shows staged progress via SSE
          ("Reading PDF... Finding topics... Checking past papers...").
      A4  All content is RAG-grounded on the student's uploaded chunks when they
          exist; ungrounded content carries an "AI-general" label.
      A5  Idempotent jobs: re-running ingestion for the same document hash must
          not duplicate rows.
      A6  Cost guard: per-user daily token budget; cheap model for extraction and
          classification, strong model for planning rationale, verification and
          note writing.
      A7  Everything user-visible is versioned: plan_v1, plan_v2 (after replan)
          so the student can see "why the plan changed".

05.4  LATENCY TARGETS (STANDARD MODE, 30-page PDF total)
      Upload -> "processing" visible          < 1 s
      Ingestion (parse+chunk+embed)           < 45 s
      Topic extraction + PYQ mapping          < 40 s
      Triage + plan                           < 15 s
      First notes (top 3 topics) streamed     < 30 s after plan
      Full notes + cheat sheet (Pass Core)    < 3 min, streamed progressively
      Student can start the first block before everything finishes (progressive
      delivery is a requirement).

[S06] TECH STACK AND INFRASTRUCTURE DECISIONS
================================================================================

06.1  STACK (chosen; do not change without logging a decision)
      Frontend      Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript + Tailwind + shadcn/ui
      State/data    TanStack Query + Zustand (UI state only)
      Validation    Zod (shared between client, API and agents)
      Backend       Next.js Route Handlers + Supabase (Postgres, Auth, Storage,
                    Realtime, Edge Functions)
      Vector search pgvector inside Supabase Postgres
      Worker        Node worker (Railway/Fly/Render or Supabase Edge Function)
                    reading the `jobs` table. Keeps ingestion off the web tier.
      LLM           Provider abstraction `llm.ts` (default OpenAI API).
                    Tiers: FAST (extraction/classification), STRONG (planning
                    rationale, note writing, verification), EMBED (embeddings).
                    Model names live in env vars, never in code.
      OCR           Tesseract (server) for clean printed text; vision-capable LLM
                    for phone photos, tables, diagrams, handwriting fallback.
      PDF parse     pdfjs-dist / pdf-parse for text PDFs; fall back to OCR if
                    text density per page is low (< 200 chars).
      PDF export    Headless Chromium (Playwright) rendering an HTML template ->
                    A4 PDF (cheat sheet + notes bundle).
      Diagrams      Mermaid (in notes) + simple SVG generated from JSON.
      PWA           next-pwa / Workbox; IndexedDB (Dexie) for offline content.
      Analytics     PostHog (events) - behavior signals also stored in own DB.
      Hosting       Vercel (web) + Supabase (data) + worker host.
      Sandbox (V2)  Docker for code execution.

06.2  ENVIRONMENT VARIABLES (all in .env.example, never commit secrets)
      APP_NAME, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY,
      SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY, LLM_PROVIDER, LLM_API_KEY,
      LLM_MODEL_FAST, LLM_MODEL_STRONG, LLM_MODEL_EMBED, LLM_MODEL_VISION,
      OCR_MODE (tesseract|vision), MAX_UPLOAD_MB (default 25),
      DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET_PER_USER, WORKER_CONCURRENCY, SEARCH_API_KEY (V1),
      POSTHOG_KEY.

06.3  WHY THESE CHOICES (one line each; use in Q&A)
      Supabase: auth + DB + storage + realtime + vectors in one place = fastest
      to ship. pgvector: no extra vector DB to run. Deterministic planner in
      code: reliable math, testable. PWA: students study on phones with flaky
      networks. Provider abstraction: swap models without touching agents.

      /scripts                 dev utilities
      agent.md, project.txt    (repo root)

07.2  CONVENTIONS
      - TypeScript strict. No `any` in packages/core and packages/schemas.
      - Files < 300 lines. One responsibility per file.
      - Branches: main (protected), feat/<agent>-<topic>. Small PRs.
      - Commit style: type(scope): message (feat, fix, docs, test, chore).
      - Every agent folder must contain: prompt.ts, schema.ts, run.ts, and at
        least 5 eval cases in eval/.
      - Every deterministic function in packages/core has unit tests including
        edge cases (0 time, 0 topics, all mastered, impossible target).
      - Errors: typed error classes; user-facing messages are calm and
        actionable ("We couldn't read page 4. Try a clearer photo.").
      - Logging: structured JSON; never log full document text or PII.
      - Definition of Done: see agent.md and S16 per agent.

[S09] BEHAVIOR SIGNALS AND THE LEARNING TWIN
================================================================================

09.1  PURPOSE: The student's "overall study routine or behavior" personalizes the
      plan. For first-time users we only have self-report + a 3-minute
      micro-diagnostic; for returning users the Learning Twin accumulates real
      data. Design so day-1 users still get a useful plan.

09.2  THE 8-QUESTION BEHAVIOR CHECK (tap-based, < 90 seconds)
      Q1  How long can you focus before your mind drifts?
          (<15 / 15-25 / 25-40 / 40+ min)                -> focus_span_min
      Q2  When do you think best? (morning / afternoon / evening / night)
                                                          -> chronotype
      Q3  When you study, what works best? (videos / reading / practice
          questions / examples / explaining to someone)  -> prefers
      Q4  How much of the syllabus have you touched at all? (0 / <25 / 25-50 /
          50+ %)                                          -> prior_coverage
      Q5  How do you feel right now? (calm / nervous / very anxious / blank)
                                                          -> panic_index seed
      Q6  Phone distraction level while studying? (low / medium / high)
                                                          -> distraction
      Q7  Hours you can REALLY give before the exam (excluding sleep/meals)?
                                                          -> declared_hours
      Q8  Which subject type is hardest for you? (memorizing / numericals /
          diagrams / long answers / theory)               -> weak_style

09.3  THE 5-QUESTION MICRO-DIAGNOSTIC
      Generated by R10 from the top-5 highest expected-marks topics (from S11).
      Format: MCQ / one-line answers. Time-boxed to 3 minutes. Measures: mastery
      seed per topic, speed (ms per item), confidence calibration (self-rated
      confidence vs correctness).
      Skippable, but skipping lowers Pass Confidence certainty (wider sigma).

09.4  EVENTS TO LOG (table `events`)
      session_start/stop, block_start/complete/skip/overrun, tab_blur/focus,
      idle_gt_60s, card_view(time_ms), quiz_answer(time_ms, correct, hints),
      panic_pressed, replan_triggered, translate_used, note_layer_switch,
      cheatsheet_opened, sleep_block_shortened, feedback_thumb.

09.5  DERIVED METRICS (computed in packages/core/twin.ts; deterministic)
      focus_span_min     = median uninterrupted block time (blur-adjusted),
                           blended with self-report (weight shifts to observed
                           as n_sessions grows).
      speed_factor       = clamp(baseline_ms / observed_ms, 0.6, 1.5) per
                           difficulty band.
      retention_est      = accuracy on recall items 2h+ after learn block.
      plan_adherence     = completed_planned_min / planned_min (rolling).
      overrun_ratio      = actual_min / planned_min (per block type).
      procrastination_ix = seconds from block-ready to block-start (normalized).
      panic_index        = f(self-report, panic button presses, rapid skipping,
                           quiz accuracy drop at night). 0..1.
      chronotype_fit     = accuracy by time-of-day bucket.
      calibration        = mean(|self_confidence - correctness|).
      Update rule: exponential moving average, alpha = 0.3 per session; cold-
      start priors come from self-report; store version history.

09.6  HOW THE TWIN CHANGES THE PLAN (explicit mappings; implement in S13)
      focus_span short        -> shorter blocks (15/5), more breaks, more variety
      chronotype night        -> hardest topics placed in evening window
      speed_factor low        -> more minutes per topic; smaller Pass Core
      panic_index high        -> smaller visible task set, calmer copy, early
                                 easy win first, 60-sec breathing block
      prefers examples        -> notes lead with worked examples
      prefers practice        -> recall quizzes before reading (test-first)
      weak_style numericals   -> extra worked-problem blocks, formula sheet
                                 emphasized
      distraction high        -> shorter blocks, "phone away" prompts, no long
                                 video resources
      calibration poor        -> more quiz evidence before trusting mastery

09.7  PRIVACY: Twin data is the user's. Provide "View my Twin" and "Delete my
      Twin" in settings (S24).

[S10] INGESTION PIPELINE (PDF / IMAGES / TEXT)
================================================================================

10.1  PIPELINE STAGES (worker jobs; each stage idempotent via sha256 + stage flag)

      STAGE 0  VALIDATE
        - Allowed: pdf, png, jpg, jpeg, webp, txt, md, docx (V1), pptx (V1).
        - Max size MAX_UPLOAD_MB, max pages 300 per doc (MVP).
        - Virus/malware scan hook (V1) and MIME sniffing (never trust extension).
        - Compute sha256; if same hash exists for user+exam -> reuse.

      STAGE 1  EXTRACT TEXT
        - Text PDF: extract per page. If avg chars/page < 200 -> treat as scan.
        - Scan/image: OCR (Tesseract; if confidence < 0.6 or layout is a table/
          diagram/handwriting -> vision-LLM transcription).
        - Preserve: page numbers, headings (by font size/position), lists,
          tables (as markdown tables), math (LaTeX-ish where possible).
        - Output: pages[] {page_no, text, ocr_conf, blocks[]}

      STAGE 2  CLEAN
        - Remove headers/footers repeated on > 60% of pages, page numbers,
          watermarks. De-hyphenate line breaks. Normalize unicode, quotes.
        - Detect language per page (en/ml/hi/ta...). Keep original + flag.

      STAGE 3  CLASSIFY DOCUMENT (R2 with FAST model, 1 call on first pages)
        - kind: syllabus | pyq | notes | textbook | other
        - meta: year, exam name, paper code, total marks, sections detected.

      STAGE 4  CHUNK
        - Semantic chunks by headings; 300-500 tokens target, 50 token overlap;
          never split a table, worked example, or question in the middle.
        - PYQ documents: chunk PER QUESTION (regex + LLM boundary check).

      STAGE 5  EMBED + STORE
        - Batch embed (LLM_MODEL_EMBED). Store in document_chunks with page range.

      STAGE 6  STRUCTURE EXTRACTION (R3)
        - Syllabus -> topic tree (units, topics, subtopics).
        - PYQ -> pyq_questions rows (marks, type, section, year).
        - Notes/textbook -> topic-to-chunk mapping (which chunks teach which topic).

      STAGE 7  QUALITY GATE
        - If extraction failed for > 30% of pages: mark doc "needs attention",
          tell user which pages failed and offer "retake photo" / "paste text".
        - Never silently continue with garbage. Confidence is lowered visibly.

10.2  MULTI-SOURCE MERGING
      - Topic normalization: cluster titles with embeddings + fuzzy matching,
        create canonical topic `key` (e.g. "normalization-dbms"), map aliases.
      - Conflicting mark values: trust the most recent PYQ; store all.

10.3  NO-DOCUMENTS FALLBACK
      If the student uploads nothing: R3 pulls the topic tree from `curricula`
      by board/course/subject; if absent, generate a provisional syllabus with
      the STRONG model and label unverified=true. Student must tap "Looks right"
      or edit topics before triage.

10.4  PROGRESS EVENTS (stream to UI)
      uploaded -> parsing (n/N pages) -> cleaning -> classifying -> indexing ->
      extracting topics -> mapping past papers -> ready.

10.5  FAILURE HANDLING TABLE
      Encrypted PDF        -> ask user to unlock or paste text.
      Blurry photo         -> flag pages with ocr_conf < 0.5, suggest retake.
      Huge file            -> ask to upload only relevant chapters/years.
      Non-English pages    -> process; translate summaries to English for
                              topic keys; keep source language for quoting.
      Duplicate upload     -> reuse, tell user.

================================================================================
[S11] EXAM INTELLIGENCE (TOPIC EXTRACTION, PYQ ANALYTICS)
================================================================================

11.1  GOAL: Convert raw documents into a numeric model of the exam:
      for each topic t -> p_appear, avg_marks, complexity, prerequisites,
      likely question forms.

11.2  TOPIC TREE
      - Source of truth order: syllabus > teacher notes > textbook TOC >
        curriculum library > inferred from PYQs.
      - Each topic: key, title, unit, complexity (1-5: 1 definition-level,
        3 multi-step concept, 5 heavy derivation/numerical).
      - Prerequisites via topic_edges. Mark `hard` when the topic is unlearnable
        without the prerequisite.

11.3  PYQ -> TOPIC MAPPING
      - For each question: R3 assigns 1-3 topic_ids with confidence.
      - Low-confidence mappings (< 0.6) are shown to the student for one-tap
        confirmation ("Is this question about Normalization?") only if it
        affects a top-10 topic. Otherwise ignore.

11.4  STATS (deterministic, packages/core/scoring.ts)
      Let papers = list of PYQ papers (years). n = count of papers.
      recency weight for paper year y:  w_y = 0.5 ^ ((current_year - y) / 3)
      recency_weighted_freq f_t = ( sum over papers of w_y * appeared_y(t) )
                                  / ( sum over papers of w_y )
      appeared_y(t) = 1 if any question in paper y maps to t, else 0
      avg_marks_t   = mean marks of questions mapped to t (fallback: section
                      typical marks)
      prior_t       = syllabus importance prior (unit weight in syllabus, or
                      1 / total_topics when unknown)
      emphasis_t    = 1 if student/teacher flagged "important", else 0
      p_appear_t    = clamp( 0.70*f_t + 0.20*prior_norm_t + 0.10*emphasis_t, 0.02, 0.98 )
      Small-sample smoothing: if n < 3, blend toward prior:
        p_appear_t = ( n*p_appear_t + (3-n)*prior_norm_t ) / 3
      expected_marks_t = p_appear_t * avg_marks_t
      Repetition patterns: flag topics that appear in ALTERNATE years or every
      year ("evergreen"); expose as evidence badges.

11.5  EVIDENCE STRINGS (for DP5)
      Generated from data, not by the LLM. Example:
      "Appeared in 6 of 8 papers (last: 2024). Average 10 marks. Long-answer."

11.6  CHOICE-RULE HANDLING (exam pattern)
      For sections with "attempt any k of n": only the best k questions count.
      Coverage constraint (S12) ensures enough topics are covered to be able
      to attempt k questions in each section.

11.7  PREDICTED QUESTIONS (R6 uses S11 stats)
      For each Pass Core topic produce up to 3 predicted question stems in the
      style of the past papers (type + marks), labeled "Likely question
      (estimate)". Never claim they will appear.

================================================================================
[S12] TRIAGE ENGINE ("PASS CORE" ALGORITHM)
================================================================================

12.1  PURPOSE: Choose which topics to study, at what depth, so that predicted
      score >= pass mark + safety margin using the time available. This is
      deterministic code (packages/core/triage.ts). LLM outputs feed only as
      inputs (topic complexity, mapping), never as the final selection.

12.2  DEFINITIONS
      Depth levels:
        L0  micro-prereq (10 min)    : just enough to unlock a dependent topic
        L1  SURVIVAL (cap 0.55)      : definitions, core formula/steps, one
                                        example; can score partial marks
        L2  SOLID    (cap 0.75)      : full explanation + worked example + recall
        L3  DEEP     (cap 0.90)      : derivations, edge cases, variations
      Base minutes: L0=10, L1=20, L2=45, L3=90.
      Student-adjusted minutes:
        minutes(t,d) = base(d) * complexity_t/3 * (1 - 0.6*m0_t) / speed_factor
        (clamp to [8, 150])
      Mastery after study:
        m1(t,d) = m0_t + (cap(d) - m0_t) * learn_eff      (learn_eff in [0.7,1.1],
        default 0.9; lowered if source material is poor/absent, raised if student
        has good grounded notes and high retention)
        If m0_t >= cap(d): no gain -> skip that depth.
      Gain:    G(t,d) = p_appear_t * avg_marks_t * ( m1(t,d) - m0_t )
      Density: rho(t,d) = G(t,d) / minutes(t,d)

12.3  TIME BUDGET
      T_total   = exam_start - now (minutes)
      T_fixed   = sleep + meals + travel + personal commitments (from intake)
      T_buffer  = 12% of (T_total - T_fixed)      (unexpected delays)
      T_focus   = (T_total - T_fixed - T_buffer) * efficiency
                  efficiency = focus_span-based factor: 0.75 (short span)
                  ... 0.90 (long span). Breaks are already part of efficiency.
      Sleep rule: sleep = 7h default; if T_total < 14h: 6h; if < 10h: 4.5h
      (3 sleep cycles) with warning; if < 5h: 20-min nap only. Never below
      these silently. User can override but sees a warning card.

12.4  ALGORITHM (pseudocode)

      INPUT: topics[], stats, m0[], edges, pattern, T_focus, target, twin
      1. Compute p_appear, avg_marks, expected_marks per topic (S11).
      2. Compute m0_t = blend(diagnostic result, self-report coverage, twin mastery).
      3. Build candidate options (t, d) for d in {L1, L2, L3}; compute
         minutes, gain, density.
      4. Prerequisite expansion: for each candidate with hard prereq p where
         m0_p < 0.30, add an L0 option for p and add its minutes to the
         candidate's cost (bundle).
      5. PHASE A - Survival sweep (greedy):
           sort candidates L1 by density desc; pick until T_focus * 0.75 used.
           enforce COVERAGE: for each section with attempt_k, ensure the picked
           topics can supply at least k answerable questions (marks-aware).
           If not met, force-pick the cheapest topics to satisfy coverage.
      6. Compute predicted score S (12.5). If S >= pass_mark + margin -> go to 7.
         Else: continue greedy on remaining candidates until time runs out.
      7. PHASE B - Upgrade: with remaining time, repeatedly upgrade the (t) with
         highest marginal density (L1->L2, L2->L3) while time remains and
         S < target_score. Stop when S >= target_score (do not over-study).
      8. Tag everything not picked as "Skip" with a reason string
         (low frequency / low marks / high cost / redundant / out of time).
      9. Classify picked topics: MUST (needed to reach pass), SHOULD (raises
         margin), NICE (only if time left).
      10. If S_max (max achievable) < pass_mark: return status=UNREACHABLE with
          S_max, best plan, and honest message (S03 honesty contract).
      OUTPUT: selection[{topic, depth, minutes, class, evidence}], S, sigma,
              pass_confidence, skipped[{topic, reason}], status.

12.5  PREDICTED SCORE AND PASS CONFIDENCE
      Section-wise expected score:
        For each section: take the k best expected topic-question values:
          E_q = p_appear * marks * m_final   (m_final = m1 for picked, m0 else)
        Sum over the best k (attempt_k) questions.
      Guess baseline (only if MCQs and NO negative marking):
        add 0.25 * marks_of_unanswered_mcq * 0.5  (conservative)
      Predicted score S = sum of section scores (as % of total).
      Variance per topic: var_t = marks^2 * ( p(1-p)*m^2 + p*0.04 )
      sigma = sqrt(sum var_t) (percentage points), widened by 1.25x if the
      diagnostic was skipped, and by 1.4x if no PYQ data (UNVERIFIED topics).
      Pass Confidence  PC = Phi( (S - pass_mark) / sigma )   (normal CDF)
      Bands: PC < 0.40 LOW, 0.40-0.70 MEDIUM, 0.70-0.85 GOOD, > 0.85 STRONG.
      Cap PC at 0.75 when no PYQ data. Display as e.g. "Pass Confidence: 74%
      (estimate, range 62-84%)".

12.6  EDGE CASES (must be unit tested)
      - T_focus <= 0 -> Emergency card only, plus "what to do in the last 20 min".
      - All topics already mastered -> plan = recall + cheat sheet only.
      - Target > achievable -> return best achievable with honest message.
      - PYQ missing for some years -> reduce n, smooth to prior.
      - Topic appears with 30 marks but student m0=0 and cost huge -> still
        evaluate density; if it is the only route to the pass mark, include at L1.
      - Duplicate topic titles -> merge before triage.
      - Multiple exams (V1): run triage per exam then allocate T_focus by
        marginal density across exams, honoring exam order.

================================================================================
[S13] RESCUE PLANNER (TIME-BLOCKING ALGORITHM)
================================================================================

13.1  INPUT: selection from S12, exam_start, now, twin, fixed commitments,
      sleep rule, mode (S03.2). OUTPUT: ordered plan_blocks with timestamps.
      Implemented in packages/core/planner.ts (deterministic, seeded).

13.2  BLOCK TYPES
      WARMUP        3-min "win": 3 easy flashcards from a Must topic (creates
                    momentum, lowers panic)
      LEARN         read/watch layered notes at depth d, with a done-condition
      RECALL        active recall: quiz/flashcards on the just-learned topic
      BUILD_SHEET   add the topic's essentials to the cheat sheet (student sees it)
      REVIEW        re-read cheat sheet segment + weak flashcards
      BREAK         5-15 min (adaptive), includes a suggested micro-activity
      MEAL          20-30 min, protected
      SLEEP         protected block
      FINAL_SWEEP   30-45 min before sleep: cheat sheet pass + mistake items
      MORNING_CARD  20-40 min on exam morning: panic card + 10 flashcards + exam
                    strategy (time per mark, which questions first)

13.3  SESSION SHAPE (block length from focus_span)
      focus <= 20  : 15 min work / 5 min break
      20 < f <= 35 : 25 / 5
      f > 35       : 40 / 10
      Every 4 work blocks: 15-20 min long break.
      Each LEARN block is followed within 1-3 blocks by its RECALL block.

13.4  ORDERING RULES
      R1  Start with a WARMUP block (3 min).
      R2  First real LEARN block = a Must topic that is high-density and
          low-complexity (early win), unless panic_index is low.
      R3  Topological order for prerequisites (L0 before dependents).
      R4  Place highest-complexity topics inside the student's peak window
          (chronotype/time-of-day accuracy). If exam is tomorrow morning and
          student is "night" type, hard topics go 7 pm - 11 pm.
      R5  Interleave: max 2 consecutive blocks of same unit/subject when
          multiple exist; alternate reading and practice.
      R6  Spaced passes:
            Pass 1 (learn)   all selected topics
            Pass 2 (recall)  after 2-3 blocks, then again ~2-3 h later
                             for MUST topics (expanding gap)
            Pass 3 (sweep)   FINAL_SWEEP before sleep; MORNING_CARD on the day
      R7  Numerical/diagram-heavy topics get RECALL as worked-problem practice,
          not flashcards.
      R8  Insert MEAL blocks at typical times (or user-set); keep them fixed.
      R9  Insert SLEEP as an anchor FIRST, then fill around it (anchors:
          sleep, meals, exam). Fill the remaining time slots with blocks.
      R10 Buffer: leave 10-12% unassigned as "floating buffer" blocks the
          student can use for overflow (visible, named "Catch-up").
      R11 Never schedule study in the last 30 min before leaving for the exam
          except MORNING_CARD light review.

13.5  EMERGENCY MODE (T < 4h)
      Blocks: WARMUP -> Must topics at L1 (top-density only, hard cap 6 topics)
      -> one combined RECALL -> cheat sheet REVIEW -> "Exam strategy card".
      No BUILD_SHEET blocks (cheat sheet is auto-built and ready).

13.6  EXTENDED MODE (30-72h)
      Split T_focus across days weighted 40/35/25 (or as available), Day N-1 gets
      the STANDARD structure; earlier days use Pass 1 heavy, later Pass 2/3.

13.7  OUTPUT CONTRACT (plan_blocks fields)
      {seq, type, topic_id?, depth?, start_at, planned_min, done_condition,
       why, resources?[], flags:["hard","early_win","peak_window"]}
      done_condition examples:
        LEARN  "Read the 3-minute note, then tap 'I got it' or ask for a simpler
                explanation."
        RECALL "Answer 5 questions; need 3 correct to move on."
        SLEEP  "Lights out by 23:30."

13.8  VALIDATION (planner self-check before returning)
      - Total scheduled work minutes <= T_focus.
      - No overlaps; timestamps monotonic; sleep block intact; meals intact.
      - Every selected topic has LEARN and RECALL blocks (except Emergency).
      - Plan ends >= 30 min before exam start.
      If validation fails -> auto-fix (drop lowest-density NICE topics first),
      then re-validate; if still failing throw PlannerError (never ship an
      invalid plan).

13.9  EXAMPLE (STANDARD MODE, exam 9:30 next morning, now 14:00, focus 25 min)
      14:00 WARMUP (3)              14:03 LEARN topic A L1 (25)
      14:28 BREAK (5)               14:33 RECALL A (10)
      14:43 LEARN topic B L2 (25)   ... 
      17:30 MEAL (30)               ... 
      21:30 REVIEW cheat sheet + weak cards
      22:30 FINAL_SWEEP (30)        23:00 lights-out prep
      23:15 SLEEP -> 06:15          06:45 MORNING_CARD (30)
      07:30 leave; exam 09:30
      (The real plan is generated by code; this is an illustration.)

[S14] CONTENT GENERATION SPECS
================================================================================

14.1  GROUNDING RULES (apply to all generated content)
      G1  Use retrieved chunks from the student's documents first (RAG top-k by
          topic key + embedding). Cite [doc name, p.X].
      G2  If no grounded source: content is labeled "AI-general" and goes through
          the Verifier (R13) with a stricter checklist.
      G3  Exam-relevant only: prioritize what the PYQs test and how (definition,
          derivation, numerical, diagram, compare-and-contrast).
      G4  Simple language. Short sentences. Define jargon once. Keep technical
          terms in English.
      G5  No invented facts, formulas, dates, or citations. If unsure: omit and
          flag "verify with your textbook".

14.2  NOTES - THREE LAYERS PER TOPIC
      L30s  (30-second)  : 1-line definition + 3 bullets + 1 memory hook.
      L3m   (3-minute)   : plain explanation, one worked example, formula/steps,
                           a tiny diagram (mermaid/SVG) if useful, "Common
                           mistakes" (max 3), "How to write this answer" (marks-
                           aware skeleton), 2 likely questions.
      DEEP  (optional)   : derivations, variations, edge cases, extra examples.
      Every note ends with: Sources, Verified badge (if R13 passed), and a
      "Simplify / Translate / Example" action row.

14.3  CHEAT SHEET SPEC (per subject; A4; 1 page target, 2 max)
      Sections in fixed order:
        1. PANIC CARD (top 10 lines to remember if nothing else)
        2. Formulas and key definitions (grouped by unit)
        3. Must-remember facts (dates, names, constants, rules)
        4. Mini diagrams (simplified, labelled)
        5. Mnemonics and memory hooks
        6. Answer skeletons: 2-mark / 5-mark / 10-mark structure
        7. Common traps (from mistakes + PYQ patterns)
        8. Exam-hall time strategy (minutes per mark, order of attempt)
      Layout: 2-3 column dense but readable (>= 8.5pt in print), high contrast,
      unit color-coding, tiny topic-priority stars (Must=3 stars). Two exports:
      PHONE view (single column, zoomable) and PRINT view (A4 PDF).
      The sheet GROWS as the student completes BUILD_SHEET blocks, but a full
      version is available from minute one (auto-built) so the student is never
      blocked.

14.4  FLASHCARDS
      Front/back, 1 idea per card, max 25 words each side. Types: definition,
      formula, why/how, compare, cloze, mini-numerical. Source tags (pyq id).
      Boxes 1-3 (Leitner-lite for one night): wrong -> box 1 (reshow in 15 min),
      right -> promote; done when all cards in box 3 or time up.

14.5  QUIZZES
      Kinds: diagnostic (5 items), recall (per topic 3-6), mock mini (20-30 min,
      PYQ-style, Pass Core weighted).
      Item fields: stem, options, answer, rubric (for short answers),
      misconception_tag, difficulty, marks. Auto-grade: MCQ/numeric exact;
      short answer via LLM grader with rubric + tolerance, output {score, reason};
      grader results spot-checked in evals (S23).

14.6  ANSWER TEMPLATES (marks-aware)
      2 marks : definition + 1 point/example
      5 marks : intro (1-2 lines) + 3-4 points + diagram/example + conclusion
      10 marks: intro, structured headings, diagram, example, comparison or
                application, conclusion. Include "keywords examiners look for".

14.7  EXAM-STRATEGY CARD (MORNING_CARD)
      Time per mark, order of attempt (easiest + highest expected marks first),
      what to do if blank (write definitions, draw the diagram - partial marks),
      how to avoid negative-marking losses, breathing reminder.

================================================================================
[S15] RUNTIME AGENTS (R-SERIES) - THE AI WORKERS INSIDE THE PRODUCT
================================================================================

GENERAL AGENT CONTRACT (applies to every R-agent)
  - Lives in /packages/agents/<id>-<name>/ with prompt.ts, schema.ts, run.ts, eval/.
  - Input and output are Zod-validated JSON. No free text between agents.
  - Stateless: all context passed in; results stored in agent_runs.
  - One job only. If it needs another agent's output, the Orchestrator supplies it.
  - Must return {status: ok|needs_input|failed, data, warnings[], confidence}.
  - Never invent facts. If evidence is missing: lower confidence, add a warning.
  - Tone (any user-visible text): calm, direct, short sentences, no shaming.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R0  ORCHESTRATOR ("Conductor")
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Run the correct agent graph for each user action and keep state.
  Trigger   Any API action: start_rescue, upload_done, diagnostic_done,
            block_done, quiz_done, panic, replan, finish_session.
  Inputs    session state, user action, twin snapshot.
  Outputs   next state, job list, UI events (SSE).
  Logic     Finite-state machine (S17). Fans out independent agents in parallel
            (e.g. R6 notes for topics 1..n). Retries once on failure; degrades
            gracefully (e.g., skip verification -> mark unverified).
  Rules     Owns cost/time budgets; enforces progressive delivery; writes
            agent_runs rows; never generates content itself.
  KPIs      p95 end-to-end latency (S05.4), failure rate < 2%, no orphan jobs.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R1  STUDENT PROFILER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Turn intake answers + behavior check + diagnostic results into a
            Learning Twin snapshot and initial mastery map.
  Inputs    intake form, 8 behavior answers, diagnostic attempts, prior twin.
  Outputs   twin_metrics (S09), m0 per topic, panic_index, target confirmation,
            warnings (e.g. "diagnostic skipped -> wider uncertainty").
  Tools     packages/core/twin.ts (math). LLM only for free-text goals/notes.
  Rules     Day-1 priors are self-report; label as "self-reported". Prefer
            observed data when n >= 3 sessions. Never label a student
            permanently ("weak"); use "currently".
  KPIs      Twin metrics stable across reruns (deterministic), no null fields.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R2  DOCUMENT INGESTION AGENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Parse any uploaded material into clean, page-cited chunks.
  Inputs    file(s) in storage.
  Outputs   documents row (kind, meta), pages[], chunks[] with embeddings,
            quality report (bad pages, ocr_conf).
  Tools     pdfjs, Tesseract, vision LLM, chunker, embedder.
  Rules     Follow S10 exactly. Idempotent by sha256. Never drop pages silently.
            Surface unreadable pages. Store page numbers for every chunk.
  Failure   Encrypted/blurry/huge handled per S10.5.
  KPIs      >= 95% text recall on clean PDFs; >= 85% on phone photos;
            < 45 s for 30 pages.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R3  SYLLABUS AND EXAM-PATTERN ANALYST
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Build the topic tree, exam pattern, and PYQ mapping (S11).
  Inputs    chunks (syllabus/PYQ/notes), curricula library, user hints.
  Outputs   topics[], topic_edges[], pattern{sections, marks, choice rules},
            pyq_questions[] with topic mapping + confidence.
  Model     FAST for per-question mapping, STRONG for tree consolidation.
  Rules     Canonicalize topic keys; merge duplicates; flag low-confidence
            mappings; ask the student to confirm at most 3 things
            ("needs_input") and only when it changes a top-10 topic.
            Never fabricate PYQ years or marks.
  KPIs      Topic mapping accuracy >= 85% on golden PYQ set (S23);
            zero fabricated questions.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R4  TRIAGE AGENT ("Pass Core Strategist")
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Run S12 and explain the result to the student.
  Inputs    topic_stats, m0, twin, exam target, T_focus.
  Outputs   selection[], skipped[], S, sigma, pass_confidence, status, and
            one-line WHY strings per topic (built from data, S11.5).
  Tools     packages/core/triage.ts (deterministic).  LLM only to phrase the
            summary message (calm, honest).
  Rules     The code decides; the LLM only phrases. Honesty contract (S03.7).
            If UNREACHABLE: present best achievable + safest fallback.
  KPIs      Same input -> same output (deterministic). Unit tests pass.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R5  RESCUE PLANNER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Turn selection into timestamped plan_blocks (S13) and replans (S18).
  Inputs    selection, twin, exam_start, now, fixed commitments, sleep rule.
  Outputs   plan_blocks[] (versioned), plan summary, floating buffer, warnings.
  Tools     packages/core/planner.ts, replan.ts (deterministic) + validation.
  Rules     Sleep anchor first. Validation must pass (S13.8). Writes a
            "why this changed" note on every replan.
  KPIs      0 invalid plans shipped; replan < 3 s.

R6  NOTES WRITER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Write the 3-layer notes (S14.2) for each selected topic.
  Inputs    topic, depth, retrieved chunks, PYQ questions for that topic, twin
            preferences (examples-first / practice-first), language pref.
  Outputs   notes rows (L30s, L3m, DEEP), diagram code, likely questions,
            citations [{doc,page}].
  Model     STRONG. Runs in parallel per topic; streams to UI.
  Rules     G1-G5 (S14.1). Simple language. Include "how to write this answer".
            No fabricated citations. Match depth to L1/L2/L3.
  KPIs      Faithfulness >= 95% vs source (eval), reading time matches plan.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R7  CHEAT SHEET DESIGNER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Compress notes + stats + mistakes into the cheat sheet (S14.3).
  Inputs    notes L30s/L3m, selection priorities, mistakes, PYQ patterns.
  Outputs   cheat_sheets.content JSON (sections), HTML, PDF via worker.
  Rules     Fixed section order. Fit 1 page (2 max): if overflow, cut NICE first,
            then compress wording; never reduce font below 8.5pt. Include
            PANIC CARD. Priority stars from triage. Every formula/fact must come
            from a verified note.
  KPIs      Fits target pages; 0 unverified formulas; readable on phone.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R8  RESOURCE DISCOVERY AGENT   (MVP-lite, V1 full)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Find the best 1-2 external resources per hard topic for THIS student.
  Inputs    topic, twin.prefers, level, time budget, language.
  Outputs   resources[] {url, title, type, duration, why-for-this-student}.
  Tools     web search API, approved-source list, resource graph (V1).
  Rules     MVP: only from a curated allowlist (official docs, well-known
            edu channels) and the student's own uploads. Discover -> classify
            -> validate -> rank (difficulty, quality, recency, hands-on,
            accuracy, prerequisites). Prefer <= 10-minute resources in Rescue
            Mode. Never recommend unvetted links. Never scrape paywalled data.
  KPIs      Relevance >= 4/5 on human spot check; 0 dead links.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R9  TUTOR / EXPLAINER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Answer "I don't get this" quickly, grounded on the notes.
  Inputs    student question, current topic, note context, language pref.
  Outputs   short explanation (<= 120 words), example, optional analogy,
            follow-up micro-check question.
  Modes     Simplify / Translate paragraph / Give example / Explain like I'm 12 /
            Why is this important for the exam?
  Rules     Stay within the topic. Use the student's language style. Keep
            technical terms in English with translation in brackets. If asked
            something off-syllabus: answer briefly, then return to plan.
            No lectures during Panic Mode (max 60 words).
  KPIs      Median response < 4 s; student "helpful" rate >= 80%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R10 QUIZ AND MOCK MAKER (+ GRADER)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Create diagnostic, recall and mini-mock items and grade answers.
  Inputs    topic, depth, PYQ style, mastery, misconception library.
  Outputs   quiz_items (stem, options, answer, rubric, misconception_tag,
            difficulty, marks) and grading results {score, reason, error_type}.
  Rules     PYQ-style wording. One concept per item. Distractors based on common
            misconceptions. Grader uses rubric + partial credit; for numeric,
            accept equivalent forms. Ambiguous grade -> mark "needs review" and
            be lenient. Time-box quizzes.
  KPIs      Grader agreement with human >= 90% (S23); no ambiguous MCQs.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R11 MISTAKE INTELLIGENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Explain WHY a student erred and generate the smallest fix.
  Inputs    attempt, question, correct answer, topic graph.
  Outputs   error_type (concept|recall|careless|calculation|misread|time),
            explanation (2 lines), related topics, correction micro-path
            (<= 3 steps, <= 10 min), 2 fresh practice items.
  Rules     Do not overdiagnose from one error: require >= 2 similar errors to
            label a "pattern". In Rescue Mode, corrections must fit in the plan
            (insert at most one remedial block per topic, S18).
  KPIs      Error-type agreement with human >= 80%.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R12 LEARNING TWIN UPDATER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Update and version the Twin from events and outcomes (S09.5).
  Inputs    events, attempts, block completions, session summary.
  Outputs   new twin version, deltas ("focus span 22 -> 26 min").
  Tools     packages/core/twin.ts. Runs at session end and after each block
            (lightweight).
  Rules     EMA alpha 0.3; never delete history; expose to the user (S24).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R13 VERIFIER / FACT-CHECKER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Stop hallucinations before content reaches the student.
  Inputs    generated notes/cheat sheet/quiz + the retrieved source chunks.
  Outputs   pass/fail per claim, corrections, verified=true/false.
  Checks    (1) each factual claim supported by a chunk or flagged AI-general,
            (2) formulas dimensionally and symbolically sane, (3) numbers/dates
            match sources, (4) quiz answers actually correct (solve independently),
            (5) no contradictions across notes and cheat sheet.
  Rules     Different model call than the writer; STRONG tier; "when in doubt,
            flag and remove from the cheat sheet".
  KPIs      Catches >= 90% of seeded errors in eval (S23).

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R14 CALM COACH (ANTI-OVERWHELM AGENT)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mission   Keep the student steady and moving.
  Trigger   Panic button, high panic_index, long idle, repeated skips, low
            confidence, late-night dip.
  Outputs   UI simplification flags, short supportive line, 60-sec breathing or
            reset block, "smallest next step".
  Rules     Never medical claims. No toxic positivity. Short. Offers
            "Pass Core only" view. If the student expresses serious distress
            or self-harm thoughts: stop the study flow, respond with care, and
            surface local helpline/support resources (S24).
  KPIs      Panic-to-next-action time < 60 s; user rating >= 4/5.

================================================================================
[S17] END-TO-END WORKFLOW AND STATE MACHINE
================================================================================

17.1  SESSION STATES (rescue_sessions.state)
      CREATED -> INTAKE -> UPLOADING -> INGESTING -> ANALYZING -> DIAGNOSING ->
      TRIAGED -> PLANNED -> GENERATING -> ACTIVE -> (REPLANNING <-> ACTIVE) ->
      WRAPUP -> DONE
      Side states: NEEDS_INPUT (student must confirm something), FAILED (with
      retry), PANIC (overlay, returns to ACTIVE).

17.2  STEP-BY-STEP FLOW (STANDARD MODE)

  STEP 1  INTAKE (student, ~60 s)
          Form: exam/subject, date+time, hours available, target, sleep window,
          fixed commitments. Language preference.
          Orchestrator: create exam + session. State INTAKE.

  STEP 2  UPLOAD (student, ~30-90 s)
          Drag/drop or camera capture: syllabus, PYQs, notes. Or "I don't have
          any" (fallback S10.3). State UPLOADING -> INGESTING.
          R2 runs per document in parallel; progress via SSE.

  STEP 3  BEHAVIOR CHECK (student, runs WHILE ingestion happens; ~90 s)
          The 8 questions (S09.2). Hides ingestion latency.
          R1 produces twin snapshot.

  STEP 4  ANALYZE (system, ~30-40 s)
          R3 builds topics, pattern, PYQ mapping. Stats computed (S11).
          If needs_input: show max 3 one-tap confirmations.

  STEP 5  MICRO-DIAGNOSTIC (student, ~3 min, skippable)
          R10 creates 5 items from top expected-marks topics. R1 updates m0.
          State DIAGNOSING.

  STEP 6  TRIAGE (system, < 5 s)
          R4 runs S12 -> Pass Core, skipped list, Pass Confidence.
          Student sees O1 summary and can adjust target or "I have +1 hour".

  STEP 7  PLAN (system, < 10 s)
          R5 builds plan (S13). State PLANNED. Student sees Now/Next/Later.
          Student taps "Start".

  STEP 8  GENERATE (system, parallel, progressive)
          For selection order: R6 notes (parallel per topic) -> R13 verify ->
          R10 flashcards/quizzes -> R7 cheat sheet (auto-built early, then
          refined) -> worker PDF export. First block's notes must be ready
          before block 1 begins; the rest stream in.
          State GENERATING -> ACTIVE (as soon as block 1 is ready).

  STEP 9  ACTIVE LOOP (student + system)
          For each block: show task -> student does it -> "Done" -> RECALL quiz
          -> R10 grade -> R11 mistakes -> R12 twin update -> R5 replan if
          needed (S18) -> next block. Confidence meter updates live.

  STEP 10 FINAL SWEEP + MORNING CARD
          Cheat sheet pass, weak items, exam strategy card, sleep prompt
          ("Sleep is part of the plan. Lights out by 23:15.").

  STEP 11 WRAPUP (post-exam, next day)
          Ask: "What came? Which topics? How did it feel?" Upload questions
          (optional). R12 updates twin; PYQ database enriched (with consent).
          Show "What worked / what to change next time".

17.3  PARALLELISM MAP
      [Upload ingestion] || [Behavior check]  -> join at ANALYZE
      [Notes per topic]  || [Flashcards]      -> join at CHEAT SHEET REFINE
      [PDF export] runs after cheat sheet content stable, non-blocking.

17.4  ERROR RECOVERY
      Any agent failure: retry once; if still failing, degrade:
        R6 fails -> show retrieved source snippets + "AI-general" summary.
        R13 fails -> ship content marked "unverified".
        R10 fails -> flashcards from PYQ text directly.
        R2 OCR fails -> ask for pasted text.
      Student is always told in plain words; the plan never stops.

================================================================================
[S18] LIVE ADAPTATION AND PANIC MODE
================================================================================

18.1  REPLAN TRIGGERS (deterministic rules in packages/core/replan.ts)
      T1  Cumulative overrun > 20% of planned work minutes.
      T2  RECALL score < 50% on a MUST topic.
      T3  Student skips >= 2 consecutive blocks.
      T4  Student adds/removes time ("I have 1 more hour", "I must leave at 6").
      T5  Idle > 30 min in an ACTIVE block (silent shift, gentle nudge at 15 min).
      T6  Student marks topic "I already know this" AND passes a 3-item check.
      T7  Panic Mode activated.

18.2  REPLAN RULES
      - Recompute T_focus from NOW. Recompute m0 from latest attempts.
      - Re-run triage with updated m0 and remaining time. Keep completed blocks.
      - Downgrade order when time is short: NICE dropped -> SHOULD L2->L1 ->
        SHOULD dropped -> MUST L2->L1. MUST topics are never dropped unless
        status becomes UNREACHABLE (then explain honestly).
      - T2 handling: insert at most ONE remedial block (R11 correction path,
        <= 10 min) for that topic; if it fails again, mark "cheat-sheet-only"
        (student reads the L30s note + flashcards) and move on.
      - Sleep and meals are immutable unless the user overrides with a warning.
      - Every replan creates plan_version+1 with a human-readable reason
        ("Recalculated: you finished DBMS faster than planned").

18.3  PANIC MODE (button always visible)
      UI: collapse to single card; hide timeline; Pass Core count only.
      Sequence:
        1. 60-second breathing/reset block (skippable).
        2. Show ONE smallest next step (a 5-minute task from a MUST topic).
        3. Offer "Pass Core only" plan (drops SHOULD/NICE).
        4. Calm copy from R14. Log panic_pressed (feeds panic_index).
      Exit: student completes the small step -> return to ACTIVE with a
      compressed plan.

18.4  CONFIDENCE METER
      After each RECALL: update m for the topic (Bayesian-like: m_new =
      0.6*observed + 0.4*m_prior, bounded by depth cap), recompute S and PC.
      Never show a sudden large drop without explanation; show "why".

18.5  SLEEP GUARD
      If user tries to delete the sleep block: show evidence-based message
      ("Sleep helps memory stick. We removed 2 low-value topics instead."),
      offer "keep sleep" as default action. Allow override once, with a warning.

[S19] PROMPT LIBRARY (SKELETONS; B2 refines and versions them)

GLOBAL SYSTEM PREAMBLE (prepend to every runtime agent prompt)
  "You are one specialist inside a study-rescue system for students under time
   pressure. Be accurate, calm and concise. Use only the provided sources when
   they exist and cite them as [doc, p.X]. If a fact is not in the sources,
   either omit it or label it AI-general. Never invent formulas, dates,
   questions or citations. Return ONLY valid JSON matching the schema."

P-R2 CLASSIFIER
  Task: Classify this document (syllabus|pyq|notes|textbook|other) and extract
  meta (year, exam, paper_code, total_marks, sections). Input: first 3 pages.
  Output schema: {kind, meta:{year?,exam?,paper_code?,total_marks?,sections?[]},
  confidence}. If unsure: kind="other", confidence<0.5.

P-R3 TOPIC TREE BUILDER
  Task: From syllabus text, produce a topic tree: units -> topics -> subtopics.
  For each topic: title, unit, complexity 1-5 (with one-line reason), hard
  prerequisites (by title). Merge duplicates. Do not add topics not in the
  syllabus unless flagged inferred=true.
  Output: {topics:[{key,title,unit,complexity,prereqs[],inferred}]}.

P-R3 PYQ MAPPER
  Task: For each question below, return marks, q_type, section, and 1-3 topic
  keys from the provided topic list with a confidence 0-1. If no topic fits,
  return topic_keys=[] and note. Do not rewrite the question.
  Output: {questions:[{q_number,marks,q_type,section,topic_keys[],confidence}]}.

P-R4 TRIAGE SUMMARY (phrasing only; numbers come from code)
  Task: Write a calm 3-sentence summary of this plan for a student with X hours
  left. State Pass Confidence and range exactly as provided. Mention 3 Must
  topics and 1 thing we are skipping and why. If status=UNREACHABLE, be honest,
  kind and practical. No promises of guaranteed results.

P-R6 NOTES WRITER
  Task: Write notes for topic {title} at depth {L1|L2|L3} using ONLY the source
  excerpts. Produce L30s, L3m (explanation, worked example, steps/formula,
  common mistakes <=3, how-to-write-answer for {marks} marks, 2 likely
  questions) and optional DEEP. Simple English, short sentences. Student
  prefers {examples|practice|reading}. Cite sources. If sources are thin,
  say so in warnings and mark ai_general=true for unsupported parts.
  Output: {l30s, l3m:{...}, deep?, diagram_mermaid?, citations[], warnings[]}.

P-R7 CHEAT SHEET COMPOSER
  Task: Compose the cheat sheet in the fixed section order (S14.3) from the
  verified notes. Hard limit: {pages} page(s). Priority order = triage classes.
  Cut NICE first. Keep each formula exactly as in notes (copy, don't rewrite).
  Output: {sections:[{title,items:[{text,priority,topic_key}]}], overflow_cut[]}.

P-R9 TUTOR
  Task: The student is confused about: "{question}". Current topic: {topic}.
  Answer in <= 120 words (<= 60 in Panic Mode), simple words, one example,
  then ask one tiny check question. Language: {lang}, keep technical terms in
  English. Stay grounded on the notes.

P-R10 QUIZ MAKER
  Task: Create {n} PYQ-style items for {topic} at difficulty {d}. Each item:
  stem, options (MCQ) or model answer + rubric (short), correct answer,
  misconception_tag for each wrong option, marks. One concept per item. No
  trick wording. Do not repeat these existing items: {existing}.

P-R10 GRADER
  Task: Grade the response using the rubric. Give score 0..marks, a 1-line
  reason, and error_type if wrong. Accept equivalent phrasing and units. If
  ambiguous, be lenient and set needs_review=true.

P-R11 MISTAKE ANALYZER
  Task: Given question, correct answer, student answer, and topic graph,
  classify error_type, explain in <= 2 lines, list related topics, propose a
  correction path (<= 3 steps, <= 10 min total) and 2 practice items.

P-R13 VERIFIER
  Task: Check every claim in the content against the excerpts. For each
  claim: supported | unsupported | contradicted, with the excerpt id. Solve
  each quiz item independently and compare with the given answer. Check
  formulas for symbolic consistency. Output failures with corrections.
  Be strict: unsupported facts must be removed or labeled AI-general.

P-R14 CALM COACH
  Task: The student pressed panic / seems overwhelmed. Write <= 25 words:
  acknowledge, reduce the task to one small step, no shaming, no medical
  claims. Output {message, suggested_action}.

PROMPT RULES
  - Version every prompt (semver) in docs/prompts-changelog.md.
  - Store prompt_version in agent_runs.
  - Keep few-shot examples in /packages/agents/<id>/examples.
  - Temperature: extraction/grading 0-0.2; notes 0.3; quiz creation 0.5.

[S22] SUPPORTING MODULES (LAYER B - PLATFORM)
================================================================================

22.1  ADAPTIVE LEARNING PATH (V1)
      Student -> understands student -> finds resources -> builds roadmap ->
      observes -> adapts -> evaluates -> rebuilds. Graph of skills with
      prerequisites; recompute after each assessment. Primary goal +
      temporary "Explore" topics that never overwrite the main roadmap;
      "Return to my roadmap" restores state.

22.2  TIME ENGINE (long-term)
      Given goal + days + hours/day: compute required hours vs available;
      prioritize essentials; push advanced topics to post-goal roadmap;
      honest about the gap. (Rescue Mode is the extreme, T <= 72h case.)

22.3  RESOURCE DISCOVERY ENGINE (V1)
      Learning Resource Graph. Pipeline: discover -> classify -> validate ->
      rank -> recommend. Ranking features: difficulty fit, quality, recency,
      hands-on, community feedback, accuracy, prerequisites, student ability.
      Every recommendation explains "why this for you".

22.4  COMMUNITY RESOURCES + REPUTATION (V1)
      Submission -> AI checks (relevance, accuracy, credibility, malicious
      content, duplicates, difficulty, freshness) -> human/community
      verification -> resource graph. Do NOT let AI alone decide.
      Contributor reputation = accepted ratio, community rating, accuracy
      reports, longevity. Anti-abuse: rate limits, link scanning, shadow-ban.

22.5  MISTAKE INTELLIGENCE (MVP-lite in Rescue, full in V1)
      See R11. Turns mistakes into correction paths and updates the Twin.

22.6  GAMIFIED QUEST MAP (V1)
      Milestones unlock the next area; adaptive gating ("You're ready, but your
      HTTP analysis score is 54% - try this optional challenge"). Badges never
      shame; streaks are optional and forgiving.

22.7  PERSONAL KNOWLEDGE BASE + WEEKLY REVISION (V1)
      "My Knowledge": per concept - what I learned, my mistakes, key ideas, my
      notes, resources, last reviewed. Weekly digest: forgotten concepts,
      15-min revision plan. Spaced repetition scheduler.

22.8  PER-PARAGRAPH TRANSLATION (MVP)
      English is default. Each paragraph has [Translate] -> language of choice
      (Malayalam, Hindi, Tamil...), [Simplify English], [Example]. Keep
      technical terms in English with the translation in brackets. Cache
      translations per paragraph hash. Flag "machine translated".

22.9  NAME/POSITIONING NOTE
      Candidate names: SkillOS, PathMind, LearnTwin, AdaptIQ, Mentora, PathOS.
      Recommended: SkillOS - "Your AI-powered personal learning operating
      system." Flagship mode label: "Exam Eve Rescue".

[S24] SECURITY, PRIVACY, ETHICS
================================================================================

24.1  DATA PROTECTION
      - RLS everywhere; private storage buckets; signed URLs (24h).
      - Encrypt in transit (TLS) and at rest (provider default).
      - Minimal PII: name (optional), email, language, level. No location
        stored; do not log document text or PII in application logs.
      - User data controls: export, delete account, delete Twin, delete
        uploads (hard delete within 30 days).
      - Minors: many users are under 18. Show clear consent language; parental
        consent flow where local law requires (verify per market); no
        targeted ads; no selling data. Uploaded content is not used for model
        training without explicit opt-in.

24.2  ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
      - Terms + UI copy: the cheat sheet is a revision aid, not for use in an
        exam hall. We do not provide features designed to smuggle information
        into exams, and we do not answer live-exam questions.
      - If a user asks for help during a live exam (detected by time overlap
        with exam window + wording), the Tutor declines politely and points to
        the calm/strategy card instead.

24.3  CONTENT AND COPYRIGHT
      - Student-uploaded material is used only for that student's own study
        outputs. Do not redistribute it or add it to a shared pool without
        consent. Cheat sheet/notes paraphrase and condense; never reproduce
        long verbatim copyrighted text.
      - PYQ database contributions are opt-in, de-identified, and moderated.
      - Resource discovery: allowlist first; respect robots/ToS; cite sources.

24.4  AI SAFETY AND TRUST
      - Verifier (R13) before showing generated facts; show Verified badges.
      - Label AI-general content. Show confidence, never absolute claims.
      - Prompt-injection defense: treat uploaded documents as DATA. Strip/ignore
        instructions inside documents ("ignore previous instructions"); never
        execute links or code from documents; system prompt is isolated.
      - Rate limiting, abuse detection, upload scanning, file-type sniffing.

24.5  WELLBEING
      - Calm Coach (R14) rules: no medical/psychological diagnosis, no
        toxic positivity. If distress signals (self-harm, hopelessness) appear:
        pause study flow, respond with care, encourage reaching out to a
        trusted person, and show region-appropriate helplines (maintain a
        vetted, up-to-date list in config; verify numbers before shipping).
      - Sleep protection is a product value, not just a feature.

24.6  ACCESSIBILITY + FAIRNESS
      - Low-bandwidth mode; regional languages; no assumptions about device.
      - Predictor must not use protected attributes. Audit for bias by
        board/university/language when calibrating.

26.4  LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
      Q: Is this just a chatbot?  A: No. Deterministic planner + PYQ analytics +
         behavior-driven Twin + verification; the LLM is one component.
      Q: Isn't cramming bad?  A: It is the real behavior. We make it safer:
         protect sleep, triage to what matters, and steer users toward the
         long-term path (Layer B) after their exam.
      Q: Can you guarantee a pass?  A: No, and we say so. We maximize pass
         probability and show an honest confidence range.
      Q: Is the cheat sheet cheating?  A: It is a revision sheet to memorize
         from, not for use in the exam hall; terms and UX say so.
      Q: How do you avoid hallucinations?  A: RAG on the student's own material,
         page citations, independent verifier agent, unverified labels.
      Q: Business model?  A: Free Rescue (limited), paid: unlimited exams,
         multi-exam, teacher/class packs, institution licenses.
      Q: What if no PYQs?  A: Curriculum library + provisional syllabus, marked
         unverified, confidence capped.

[S29] GLOSSARY
================================================================================

  Pass Core      Minimum set of topics/depths reaching pass mark + margin.
  PYQ            Previous-year question paper(s).
  Learning Twin  Continuously updated model of a student's knowledge, skills,
                 speed, retention, behavior and preferences.
  Depth L0-L3    Prereq micro / Survival / Solid / Deep coverage levels.
  Density        Expected marks gained per minute studied (rho).
  Pass Confidence Estimated probability of reaching the pass mark, with range.
  Sigma          Uncertainty (percentage points) of predicted score.
  Now/Next/Later Calm UI pattern showing only the current and next actions.
  Panic Mode     Simplified flow that reduces the plan to one small next step.
  RAG            Retrieval-augmented generation: answers grounded on retrieved
                 chunks of the student's own documents.
  Verified       Content that passed the Verifier (R13) against sources.
  AI-general     Content not grounded in the student's documents; labeled.
  R-series       Runtime agents inside the product (S15).
  B-series       Build agents that construct the product (S16).
  Cut line       Ordered list of features to drop first if time runs out (S04.4).
